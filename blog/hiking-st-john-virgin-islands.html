10 Best Hikes in St. John, USVI

Updated August 2026 · 10-minute read

Two-thirds of St. John is Virgin Islands National Park, which means 20-plus miles of maintained trails through tropical dry forest, past 200-year-old sugar plantation ruins, and along cliffs with views that make you forget you're still in US territory. No other Caribbean island comes close for hiking. Here are the ten best trails, ranked by a mix of scenery, interest, and "would I do it again."

Classic Trails

1. Reef Bay Trail

2.2 mi one-wayModerate3-4 hours

The signature hike on St. John and arguably the best trail in the Caribbean. A steady downhill through shaded tropical forest — bay rum trees, kapok, wild tamarind — past the ruins of four sugar estates and a spur trail to Taino petroglyphs carved into rock pools. The trail ends at Reef Bay beach. The NPS guided hike ($40, book at the visitor center) includes interpretation and a boat ride back to Cruz Bay, saving you the brutal uphill return. Without the boat, budget extra time and water for the climb back. The petroglyphs are pre-Columbian — some of the only surviving Taino rock art in the Caribbean.

2. Ram Head Trail

1.8 mi round tripModerate1.5-2 hours

Starts at Salt Pond Bay beach and climbs to a rocky headland with 360-degree views — open Caribbean to the south, St. Croix on the horizon, the BVI to the north. The trail crosses dry cactus scrubland (completely different ecosystem from the forest trails) before reaching the exposed point. Unshaded and hot — start early or go for sunset. The point itself is dramatic and vertiginous. Combine with a swim at Salt Pond before or after. One of the best sunset hikes in the USVI. See our St. John guide for the full picture.

3. Lind Point Trail

1.1 mi one-wayEasy30-45 min

Starts right behind the National Park visitor center in Cruz Bay — the most accessible trail on the island. A shaded path through coastal forest to an overlook above Caneel Bay, or continue down to Honeymoon Beach and Solomon Beach. Flat enough for sandals, short enough to do before or after a ferry. The Caneel Bay overlook gives a postcard view of the bay with St. Thomas across the water. A perfect first-morning or last-afternoon hike.

Essential hiking gear

Minimum: 1 liter of water per person per hour, sunscreen, hat, closed-toe shoes for Reef Bay and Ram Head. Optional: snorkel gear (many trails end at beaches), dry bag for electronics, insect repellent. Trail maps are free at the National Park visitor center in Cruz Bay (open 8 AM – 4:30 PM). Cell service is spotty on most trails.

Ruins and History Trails

4. Annaberg Sugar Plantation Loop

0.25 mi loopEasy20-30 min

The best-preserved sugar plantation ruins in the USVI. A short self-guided loop past a windmill tower, slave quarters, factory buildings, and a horse mill — with interpretive signs explaining the history of sugar production and enslaved labor in the Danish West Indies. Views of Leinster Bay and the BVI from the hilltop. The trailhead shares a parking lot with the Waterlemon Cay snorkel access, so combine the two. Free with your park pass. A heavy history lesson in a beautiful setting.

5. Cinnamon Bay Loop

0.5 mi loopEasy20 min

A short loop through the ruins of a Danish sugar plantation — factory walls, a rum still base, and an old cemetery — before emerging at the island's longest beach. The trail is flat, shaded, and takes less than 20 minutes. Combined with beach time at Cinnamon Bay, it makes a satisfying half-day. The ruins are partially restored with interpretive plaques. Most people walk right past the trailhead and miss it — look for the sign at the west end of the parking area.

6. Peace Hill

0.2 mi one-wayEasy10 min

A five-minute walk to the foundation of the old Windberg sugar mill, now topped with a Christ of the Caribbean statue (lost to Hurricane Maria, the foundation remains). The 360-degree view from the hilltop takes in Hawksnest Bay, Denis Bay, and St. Thomas. The shortest and easiest "hike" on this list — more of a quick detour. Park at the roadside pullout between Hawksnest and Trunk Bay. Worth the stop for the view alone.

Challenging Trails

7. Bordeaux Mountain Trail

1.2 mi one-wayStrenuous1.5-2 hours

The highest point on St. John at 1,277 feet. The trail descends steeply from the Bordeaux Mountain Road through dense moist forest — the wettest, greenest part of the island. Huge kapok trees, ferns, and birdsong. The trail connects to Lameshur Bay, a remote south-shore beach with no facilities and almost no people. The descent is steep and rocky — real hiking shoes are essential. Driving to the trailhead requires a 4WD vehicle on a rough road. The reward is the most "jungle" feeling you'll find in the USVI.

8. Brown Bay Trail

1.6 mi one-wayModerate-Strenuous2-3 hours

An east-end trail to a remote beach that almost nobody visits. The trail passes through dry forest and ends at Brown Bay — a rocky beach with excellent snorkeling and complete solitude. The return is the same path, so budget double the time. No shade on the beach, no facilities, no other people (usually). Bring everything you need including extra water. The snorkeling off the rocks at Brown Bay is some of the best on the island precisely because nobody goes there.

9. Yawzi Point Trail

0.5 mi one-wayEasy-Moderate30 min

A spur trail off the main road near Salt Pond Bay that leads to a rocky point between two bays. Views of Ram Head, Lameshur Bay, and the south shore. Less dramatic than Ram Head but far less traveled. A good add-on if you're already at Salt Pond and want a second hike without driving. The trail is mostly flat with one short rocky section. Birdwatching is excellent along this stretch — look for bananaquits, hummingbirds, and pelicans.

10. Johnny Horn Trail

1.8 mi one-wayModerate2-3 hours

Connects the Coral Bay area to Waterlemon Bay on the north shore, passing through dry forest with views of the BVI and Tortola. Less trafficked than the North Shore trails and with a different feel — drier, more open, with cactus and century plants. The trail can be combined with a snorkel at Waterlemon Cay for a full-day outing. Check in at the Emmaus Moravian Church trailhead parking. A shuttle or two-car setup avoids retracing your steps.

When to hike

December through April is ideal — dry, with temperatures in the low 80s and steady trade winds. Summer hikes are hotter and more humid, with afternoon rain showers possible. Start every hike before 9 AM regardless of season. The trails are not lit and there are no shelters — plan to finish well before dark. Check our USVI beaches guide for post-hike beach options.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best hike in St. John?

The Reef Bay Trail is the most popular and rewarding hike — a 2.2-mile downhill trail through tropical forest past sugar mill ruins and pre-Columbian petroglyphs, ending at a beach. The National Park Service offers a guided version with a boat ride back to Cruz Bay ($40). Ram Head Trail is the best for dramatic cliff views.

Are there hiking trails in Virgin Islands National Park?

Yes — Virgin Islands National Park on St. John has over 20 maintained trails totaling more than 20 miles. Trails range from easy 15-minute walks to strenuous multi-hour hikes. Most pass through tropical dry forest with views of the Caribbean, and many lead to historic sugar plantation ruins.

Do you need hiking boots for St. John trails?

For Reef Bay Trail, Ram Head, and the Bordeaux Mountain trails, closed-toe shoes with grip are recommended — the terrain is rocky and steep in places. For shorter trails like Lind Point, Cinnamon Bay Loop, and Peace Hill, sturdy sandals or trail sandals work fine. Flip-flops are not suitable for any trail.

Is it safe to hike alone in St. John?

Yes — St. John's trails are generally safe. The main risks are heat exhaustion and dehydration, not crime. Start early (before 9 AM), bring at least a liter of water per person, wear sunscreen, and tell someone your plan. Cell service is spotty on remote trails. The National Park visitor center in Cruz Bay has current trail conditions.

How many days do you need to hike St. John?

Two to three days lets you hit the major trails plus beach time. Day one: Reef Bay Trail (half day). Day two: Ram Head Trail plus Salt Pond Bay beach. Day three: Lind Point or Cinnamon Bay Loop plus North Shore beaches. A single day trip from St. Thomas gives time for one short hike and a beach.
