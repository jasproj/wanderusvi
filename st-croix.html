About St. Croix

St. Croix is the largest of the three U.S. Virgin Islands (84 sq mi) and sits 40 miles south of St. Thomas, on its own, with no ferry link to St. John. It gets a fraction of St. Thomas's cruise traffic, so the harbor towns, reefs and the Salt River bio bay stay quiet. This page is the working guide: how to get here, which town to base in, what's worth a day, and the tours you can book with live availability. For a ranked list see 15 Best Things to Do in St. Croix; for after-dark options see the USVI night activities guide.

Getting to St. Croix from St. Thomas

Three ways. Fly: Cape Air and Fly the Whale run short hops from St. Thomas (STT) to St. Croix (STX), about 25 minutes gate to gate, with several departures a day. Seaplane: the seaplane base is on the Charlotte Amalie waterfront in St. Thomas and lands in Christiansted harbor, which puts you in town rather than at the airport. Ferry: the QE IV passenger ferry runs between Gallows Bay (Christiansted) and the Charlotte Amalie waterfront, twice daily, roughly 90 minutes each way. Ferry days and times change seasonally, so check the current timetable before building a day trip around it (checked October 2026). No passport is needed; it's a domestic trip.

The two towns are 17 miles apart on a 28-mile island — a 30-minute drive. Most visitors stay near Christiansted and do Frederiksted as a half day.

Buck Island Reef National Monument

Buck Island is the one thing nearly everyone does. It's a 176-acre uninhabited island 1.5 miles off the northeast coast, surrounded by a marked underwater snorkel trail through elkhorn coral; the whole reef is a U.S. national monument, so only permitted boats can land. Half-day trips (about 3.5 hours) go out morning and afternoon from Christiansted and include snorkel gear and time on Turtle Beach; full-day trips add lunch and a second snorkel stop. Water is clearest in the morning. Bring reef-safe sunscreen — the park asks for it.

Salt River Bay and the bioluminescent bay

Salt River Bay, 15 minutes west of Christiansted, is a mangrove-lined national historical park (Columbus's crew landed here in 1493) and home to one of the Caribbean's few year-round bioluminescent bays. Daytime kayak tours run about 2–3 hours through the mangroves and over the reef; night tours go out on moonless evenings when the dinoflagellates glow brightest — clear-bottom kayaks make it easier to see. Book the night tour around the new moon and expect to paddle 60–90 minutes.

Point Udall

The easternmost point of the United States, a 25-minute drive east of Christiansted past Cramer's Park. There's a stone sundial monument, a short trail down to Jack and Isaac Bay (two undeveloped beaches with good snorkeling in calm weather), and no services, so bring water. Sunrise here is the local tradition; it's also a reliable spot to watch the trade-wind swell.

Rum distilleries

St. Croix makes two rums you'll see everywhere in the USVI. Cruzan Rum (Frederiksted side, est. 1760) runs short guided tours with tastings Monday–Friday; Captain Morgan has a larger visitor center with a tour and tasting near the airport. Both are an easy add-on to a west-end day; check hours before you drive out, as both close on some holidays.

Beaches

Cane Bay (north shore) — the best shore snorkeling and the famous "wall" dive site 100 yards out; a beach bar and dive shop on site. Rainbow Beach (Frederiksted) — calm, sandy, west-facing for sunset, paddleboard rentals. Sandy Point (southwest) — a leatherback turtle refuge, open to the public only on weekend daylight hours outside nesting season. Jack and Isaac Bay (east end) — hike-in, no shade, worth it for the empty sand. Protestant Cay — the small island in Christiansted harbor, a two-minute ferry from the boardwalk.

St. Croix travel questions

How do I get to St. Croix from St. Thomas?

Fly (Cape Air or Fly the Whale, about 25 minutes, several times a day), take the seaplane from the Charlotte Amalie waterfront into Christiansted harbor, or ride the QE IV passenger ferry between Gallows Bay and Charlotte Amalie, twice daily and roughly 90 minutes. Ferry schedules change seasonally; confirm before you plan a day trip.

What are the best things to do in St. Croix?

Snorkel the Buck Island reef trail, kayak the Salt River bio bay at night, walk Christiansted's Danish forts and boardwalk, dive or snorkel Cane Bay and the Frederiksted Pier, tour the Cruzan and Captain Morgan distilleries, and drive out to Point Udall for sunrise. The ranked list is in our 15 Best Things to Do in St. Croix.

Is St. Croix or St. Thomas better?

St. Thomas is the easier first trip: more flights, taxis, shopping and the St. John ferry. St. Croix is quieter and larger, with better shore diving, a bioluminescent bay and far fewer cruise passengers, but you'll want a rental car. Many people do St. Thomas first and St. Croix on a return visit.

Does St. Croix have a bioluminescent bay?

Yes. Salt River Bay on the north shore glows year-round. Night kayak tours in clear-bottom kayaks run on dark-moon evenings and last about two hours.

Do I need a passport for St. Croix?

No. St. Croix is a U.S. territory, so U.S. citizens need only a government ID. U.S. phone plans work without roaming.

How many days do you need in St. Croix?

Three full days covers Buck Island, Salt River, both towns and the west-end beaches. Add a fourth for the Cane Bay wall dive or a day at Point Udall and the east-end bays.
