Magens Bay, St. Thomas: Everything You Need to Know

Updated October 2026 · WanderUSVI Tours you can book here →

Magens Bay is the beach that put St. Thomas on the map. Tucked into the island's north shore, it curves nearly a mile around a sheltered lagoon, with water so calm it barely moves on most days. The setting — hills rolling down to the sand, thick vegetation framing both ends — is the reason travel publications have cited it among the Caribbean's most beautiful beaches for decades.

What makes it work is the shape. The bay is a near-perfect horseshoe, with two rocky headlands blocking swell from the open Atlantic. That means the water is flat even on days when the south side of the island is choppy. Families with young kids, swimmers who want to go long, couples who just want to float — the conditions suit everyone.

The Beach Itself

The sand runs for roughly three-quarters of a mile, with the widest section in the middle where most visitors set up. Chairs and umbrellas are available for rent. There are changing rooms, showers, restrooms, and a snack bar near the parking area. The facilities are clean and well maintained — the Magens Bay Authority, which operates the park, has kept the grounds in good shape since taking over stewardship.

The water is shallow for a long stretch from shore, which is part of why it's so popular with families. Even 50 yards out, many adults are still standing. The gentle slope makes for easy wading and relaxed swimming without the strong currents you'd encounter on the island's open-coast beaches.

History of the Park

Magens Bay is a public park because of a 1946 donation. Arthur S. Fairchild, an American businessman who had been visiting and investing in St. Thomas since the 1920s, deeded the property to the people of St. Thomas in perpetuity. The condition was that it be maintained as a public recreational area — which it has been. That act is why one of the Caribbean's finest beaches charges a modest entrance fee rather than sitting behind a resort gate.

Practical Information

There is a small per-person entrance fee to the beach (paid at the gate). Parking is separate. The drive from Charlotte Amalie crosses the ridge on Skyline Drive — about 3 miles, 15 minutes by car. From the ridge, you get the first look down into the bay, which is one of the better views on the island. Safari taxi vans (the shared open-air taxis that are the standard transit on St. Thomas) run the route regularly from the main taxi stand near the cruise ship terminals.

Timing matters. When a cruise ship or two is docked in Charlotte Amalie, Magens Bay fills up. Arrive before 10 a.m. or wait until mid-afternoon, and you'll have significantly more space. The beach is quieter on Sundays, when cruise traffic is lighter.

Local tip: The bar at the far east end of the beach (away from the main entrance) is less crowded and has the same views. Walk past most of the day-trippers and you'll find a more relaxed corner of the bay.

What's Nearby

The drive back to Charlotte Amalie on Skyline Drive passes the lookout over Charlotte Amalie Harbor — stop for a few minutes, especially late afternoon when the light hits the red rooftops and the harbor islands. Coki Beach, known for its snorkeling and proximity to Coral World Ocean Park, is on the northeast shore and makes a logical second stop on a beach day.
