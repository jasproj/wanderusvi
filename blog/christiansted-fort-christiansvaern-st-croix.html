Christiansted & Fort Christiansvaern, St. Croix

Updated October 2026 · WanderUSVI Tours you can book here →

Christiansted is the kind of town that rewards the visitor who walks rather than drives. The historic district — a compact grid of colonial buildings running along the north-shore harbor — is one of the most intact Danish colonial waterfronts in the Western Hemisphere. The fort at its center is painted bright ochre-yellow, the same color the Danes applied to military buildings across their Caribbean territories. On a sunny morning, with the harbor sparkling beyond the ramparts, the visual effect is hard to forget.

Fort Christiansvaern

Construction of Fort Christiansvaern began in 1738, and the main structure was complete by 1749. The Danish built it on the harbor's east end to command the approaches to the Christiansted anchorage — its cannons pointed seaward, its thick walls able to withstand bombardment from the water. The fort served as the seat of Danish colonial administration: courthouse, government offices, military garrison, and jail all occupied its spaces at various points.

Today it is part of Christiansted National Historic Site, managed by the National Park Service. Admission is charged. Inside, the rooms have been restored and interpreted to show different eras of the fort's use — the officers' quarters, the dungeon cells, the courtroom, and the battery platforms. Rangers lead tours and can contextualize the history of Danish rule in the West Indies in ways the signs alone don't quite capture. The views from the upper ramparts over the harbor are excellent.

The Historic District

The fort anchors a walkable historic district that extends for several blocks inland. The Scale House, immediately adjacent to the fort, is where goods were weighed for customs duties during the plantation era. The Steeple Building — a former Dutch Reformed church built in 1753 — now houses a museum of pre-Columbian artifacts and colonial history. Government House, a few blocks from the waterfront, is an 18th-century mansion that served as the official residence of Danish governors and is still used for government functions today.

The waterfront boardwalk connects these sites and runs west past the small boats and kayak rental stands to a cluster of restaurants and bars. The architecture along the boardwalk is a mix of well-preserved colonial buildings and modest commercial additions — the bones of the old town are visible even where later renovation has been less careful. The covered arcades (known as "galleried storefronts") that line several streets were designed to provide shade in the tropical heat — a practical feature that also makes walking through the town more pleasant.

Alexander Hamilton

Christiansted has an unexpected American history connection: Alexander Hamilton spent several of his formative years on St. Croix, working as a clerk in the Christiansted trading firm of Nicholas Cruger. He left for New York and eventually became the first US Secretary of the Treasury. A historical marker in town notes the connection.

Local tip: The Christiansted waterfront is best in the morning before the heat builds. The boardwalk restaurants are good for breakfast or an early lunch. After the historic district, the boat dock for Buck Island charters is right on the waterfront — most operators run morning departures, making the historic district walk a natural prelude.
