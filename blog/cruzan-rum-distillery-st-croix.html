Cruzan Rum Distillery, St. Croix

Updated October 2026 · WanderUSVI Tours you can book here →

St. Croix has been producing rum since the plantation era. Sugar and rum were inseparable in the Danish West Indies — molasses, the byproduct of sugar refining, was the raw material for distillation, and virtually every large estate ran a still alongside its sugar mill. The Cruzan Rum Distillery at Estate Diamond is the continuation of that tradition, now operating as a modern distillery with public tours on one of the Caribbean's oldest rum-producing sites.

The Distillery Tour

The guided tour walks visitors through the full production process. It starts at the molasses tanks — large steel vessels where the raw fermentation material is stored before processing. From there, the route moves through the fermentation room, where molasses is mixed with water and yeast and left to convert sugars to alcohol over several days. The column stills, which distill the fermented liquid to a high-proof spirit, are the industrial center of the facility — impressive in scale in a way that pot-still operations typically aren't.

The aging warehouses are the most atmospheric stop on the tour. Stacked American oak barrels, absorbing the warmth and humidity of the Caribbean climate, are where the raw distillate becomes rum. The climate here accelerates aging — the heat drives the spirit in and out of the wood more aggressively than it would in a cooler continental location, which means Caribbean rums often develop complexity faster than whiskeys aged in Scotland or Kentucky.

The tour ends in the tasting room, where guests try several expressions from the Cruzan range. The light rum, the aged rum, and usually one specialty release are included in the standard tour. The tasting room also sells bottles at distillery prices, including some expressions not widely available outside the island.

The Name

Cruzan takes its name from "Crucian" — the local term for a person from St. Croix. The island's name comes from the French "Île de Sainte-Croix" (Island of the Holy Cross), and the people who have lived here for generations call themselves Crucians. The rum's name is a phonetic spelling of that identity.

Practical Information

The distillery is located at Estate Diamond on St. Croix's south-central coast, roughly equidistant between Christiansted and Frederiksted (about 7-10 miles from either town). Tours run most weekdays and some weekend mornings — check the current schedule before making a special trip, as hours can vary by season. A rental car is the most practical way to get there. Several island tour operators include the distillery as a stop on half-day or full-day island tours.

Local tip: The St. George Village Botanical Garden is about 2 miles from the distillery on the same road. The two make a natural combination — the garden's sugar plantation ruins and the distillery's production story are two sides of the same agricultural history.
