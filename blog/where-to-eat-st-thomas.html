Where to Eat in St. Thomas: 18 Best Restaurants and Local Food

Updated August 2026 · 9-minute read

The food scene on St. Thomas runs deeper than the resort buffets suggest. West Indian home cooking, Caribbean-French fusion in a converted fishing village, waterfront seafood with views of the harbor, and street food that's better than most sit-down meals — it's all here. This guide covers the best spots by neighborhood, from cruise-port-convenient Charlotte Amalie to the locals-only corners most visitors miss.

Charlotte Amalie

1. Gladys' Cafe

Royal Dane Mall$$Local favorite

A Charlotte Amalie institution. West Indian home cooking in a courtyard tucked behind the waterfront shops — oxtail stew, curried chicken, kallaloo soup, and conch fritters that set the standard. The hot sauce is house-made and legendary. Lunch only, closes by 3 PM most days. Walking distance from the cruise port. Cash preferred. The portions are enormous and the prices are fair by island standards.

2. Petite Pump Room

Waterfront$$$Fine dining

Upscale lunch and dinner in a historic building on the waterfront. French-Caribbean menu — seared tuna, lobster risotto, duck breast with mango glaze. The wine list is serious. Reservations recommended for dinner. One of the few Charlotte Amalie restaurants that feels like a destination rather than a convenience. Great for a splurge meal when a cruise ship hasn't just dumped 3,000 people onto Main Street.

3. Mafolie Restaurant

Mafolie Hill$$-$$$Best view

Perched on a hillside above Charlotte Amalie with panoramic harbor views — the best dinner view on the island. Caribbean seafood, steaks, and pasta. The sunset from the terrace is worth the taxi fare up the hill ($10 from downtown). Dinner reservations recommended in high season. The conch chowder and catch-of-the-day are reliable choices. Dress code is resort casual.

4. Natural Livity Juice Bar

Market Square$Vegan/vegetarian

Fresh juices, smoothies, and plant-based Caribbean food near Market Square. Ital stew, veggie roti, and juice blends with local fruit — soursop, passion fruit, guava. A genuine local spot that most tourists walk right past. Cash only, tiny, and popular at lunch. The best option for vegetarians and anyone who wants real fruit instead of a frozen daiquiri.

Cruise passenger tip

Skip the restaurants immediately at the cruise dock — they charge tourist prices for mediocre food. Walk two blocks inland toward Main Street for better options. Gladys' Cafe and the food vendors near Market Square are the best values within walking distance. Check our things to do in St. Thomas guide for orientation.

Frenchtown

5. Oceana Restaurant

Frenchtown$$$Seafood

The best seafood restaurant on St. Thomas. Fresh catch prepared with Caribbean-Mediterranean flair — grilled wahoo, lobster tail, ceviche, and a raw bar. Waterfront setting in the old French fishing village. Reservations essential in season. The chef sources fish from local boats — what's on the menu depends on what came in that morning. The prix fixe tasting menu ($75) is the best way to experience it.

6. Hook, Line & Sinker

Frenchtown$$Casual waterfront

Casual waterfront bar and grill with solid fish sandwiches, fish tacos, and burgers. Nothing fancy — picnic tables, paper plates, cold beer. The kind of place where fishermen eat after work. Friday happy hour draws a local crowd. Prices are reasonable by island standards ($12-20 for lunch). The fish sandwich with a Presidente is the move.

7. Bella Blue

Frenchtown$$-$$$Italian

Italian restaurant in a casual waterfront setting. Handmade pasta, wood-fired pizza, and fresh seafood with Italian preparations. The lobster linguine is a local favorite. Good wine selection. More relaxed than Oceana but still a proper dinner out. Frenchtown is a 5-minute taxi ride from Charlotte Amalie — the whole neighborhood is walkable once you're there.

Red Hook

8. Fish Tails

Red Hook$$Marina views

Overlooking the Red Hook marina with views of St. John across the water. Fresh seafood, tacos, and a solid bar program. Popular with the sailing crowd and anyone catching a ferry. The poke bowl and blackened mahi tacos are standouts. Live music some evenings. A good lunch stop before or after the St. John ferry. Sit on the deck if you can.

9. Duffy's Love Shack

Red Hook$$Tiki bar

A tiki-themed bar with enormous frozen drinks served in novelty containers, plus surprisingly good food — jerk chicken, fish tacos, loaded nachos. It's loud, fun, and unapologetically touristy, but the locals go too. The "Shark Tank" cocktail is a shared fishbowl that feeds four. Not the place for a quiet dinner. Perfect for the first or last night of a trip.

10. XO Bistro

Red Hook$$$Date night

Intimate bistro with a French-Caribbean menu — duck confit, pan-seared snapper, steak frites. Small space, maybe 12 tables, so reservations are essential. The chef-owner changes the menu seasonally based on what's available. One of the best restaurants on the East End and easily missed if you don't know it's there. A genuine date-night spot in a town that's otherwise casual.

Local Street Food

11. Pates

Island-wide$3-5Must-try

The USVI's signature street food — deep-fried turnovers filled with seasoned saltfish, chicken, beef, or conch. The dough is slightly sweet, the filling savory and peppery. Every bakery and food truck has them. The best versions have a flaky, golden crust and a generous filling. Try saltfish first — it's the classic. Available all day but best fresh in the morning when bakeries open.

12. Johnnycakes

Island-wide$2-4Breakfast staple

Fried cornmeal bread — crispy and golden outside, soft and dense inside. Eaten plain for breakfast or as a side with stew. Some places split them and fill them like a sandwich. Simple, cheap, filling, and genuinely delicious. Find them at any bakery or food truck. Pair with a pate and a bush tea for the full local breakfast experience.

13. Conch Fritters

Island-wide$8-12Appetizer

Deep-fried balls of conch meat mixed with peppers, onion, and seasoning. Served with a dipping sauce — usually a spicy mayo or cocktail sauce. Every beach bar and most restaurants serve them as an appetizer. Quality varies widely — the best are light and crispy with big chunks of conch, not doughy filler. Gladys' Cafe and Oceana both do excellent versions.

14. Fungi and Kallaloo

Local restaurants$10-15Traditional

The national dish of the USVI. Fungi (FOON-gee) is a cornmeal-and-okra side — similar to polenta but with a Caribbean twist. Kallaloo is a thick soup made from leafy greens, okra, and often crab or fish. Together they're a meal. You won't find this at tourist restaurants — look for local lunch spots and food trucks, especially near Market Square and in the Sub Base neighborhood.

Budget eating tip

Food trucks and roadside stands serve full meals for $8-15 — fraction of restaurant prices. The food truck cluster near Tutu Park Mall has rotating options. Market Square in Charlotte Amalie has vendors during the day. Ask for "local plate" or "plate lunch" — you'll get a protein, rice and peas, coleslaw, and plantains for around $12.

Around the Island

15. Havana Blue

Morning Star Beach$$$Cuban-Asian fusion

Cuban-Asian fusion at the Marriott's Frenchman's Reef — creative cocktails, ceviche flights, and dishes that blend Caribbean heat with Asian technique. The beachfront setting is stunning at sunset. One of the most Instagram-worthy restaurants on the island for good reason. Reservations recommended. Pricey but the food backs it up. The tuna tartare and Cuban pork belly are signatures.

16. Room with a View

Bluebeard's Castle$$$Panoramic

Perched above Charlotte Amalie at the historic Bluebeard's Castle hotel. The view is exactly what the name promises — the entire harbor, cruise ships, and Charlotte Amalie spread below. Caribbean-continental menu, strong cocktails, and a setting that makes even average food taste better. Dinner only. The sunset is the real main course. Worth a taxi up the hill.

17. Coki Beach Bar

Coki Beach$-$$Beach bar

Feet-in-the-sand bar right on the island's best snorkeling beach. Rum punches, beer, grilled chicken, and fish plates. Nothing refined — paper plates, plastic cups, reggae music. But you're eating 20 feet from turquoise water with fish swimming beneath you. The rum punch is strong and cheap. The fish plate ($12-15) is fresh. That's all you need.

18. Mountain Top

St. Peter Mountain$$Banana daiquiri

Claims to have invented the banana daiquiri — and after trying one on the terrace 2,100 feet above sea level with views stretching to the BVI, you won't argue. It's touristy (every taxi tour stops here), but the view is genuinely spectacular and the banana daiquiri is genuinely good. Light lunch menu — burgers, wraps, and nachos. Come for the drink and the view, not a serious meal.

Frequently Asked Questions

What food is St. Thomas known for?

St. Thomas is known for Caribbean seafood, especially conch (fritters, chowder, and salad), fresh-caught mahi-mahi and snapper, and local street food like pates (fried meat turnovers) and johnnycakes (fried cornmeal bread). Kallaloo soup, fungi (cornmeal with okra), and saltfish are traditional West Indian staples you'll find at local spots.

Is food expensive in St. Thomas?

Restaurant meals range from $12-20 at casual spots to $30-60 at waterfront restaurants. Food truck and street food meals run $5-12. Groceries cost 30-50% more than the mainland US since most items are shipped in. Budget tip: eat lunch at food trucks and splurge on one nice dinner.

Where should cruise ship passengers eat in St. Thomas?

Walking distance from the cruise port: Gladys' Cafe and Mafolie Restaurant in Charlotte Amalie for local food, or Petite Pump Room for upscale lunch. Taxi to Red Hook (20 min) for Fish Tails and Duffy's Love Shack. Avoid the tourist traps immediately at the cruise dock — walk two blocks inland for better food and prices.

Do I need reservations for restaurants in St. Thomas?

For dinner at popular waterfront spots (Havana Blue, Room with a View, Oceana), yes — especially December through April. Most casual restaurants and all food trucks are walk-in. Lunch rarely requires reservations anywhere. Call the same day for most places; a week ahead for peak-season dinner at the top restaurants.

What is a pate in the Virgin Islands?

A pate (pronounced PAH-tay) is a deep-fried turnover filled with seasoned meat — usually saltfish, chicken, beef, or conch. It is the signature street food of the US Virgin Islands. The dough is slightly sweet, the filling is savory. You'll find them at bakeries, food trucks, and roadside stands across St. Thomas for $3-5 each.
