15 Best Things to Do in St. John, USVI

Updated August 2026 · 11-minute read

St. John is the smallest of the three main US Virgin Islands and, by a wide margin, the wildest. Two-thirds of it is national park — tropical forest, coral reefs, sugar plantation ruins, and beaches that routinely top "best in the world" lists. No airport, no cruise ship dock, no chain hotels. You get here by ferry, and the pace shifts the moment the boat pulls away from Red Hook. Here's everything worth doing.

Beaches

1. Trunk Bay

North Shore$5 NPS feeUnderwater trail

The most photographed beach in the Caribbean. A wide arc of white sand with green hills behind and an underwater snorkeling trail marked by plaques identifying coral and fish. Lifeguards, restrooms, showers, and a snack bar. Gets busy by noon — take the first ferry and a taxi straight here. The $5 National Park fee covers all park beaches for the day. Worth the hype. See our best USVI beaches guide for more detail.

2. Cinnamon Bay

North ShoreFree w/ park passLongest beach

Half a mile of sand — the longest beach on St. John. Excellent snorkeling on the east side reef, windsurfing rentals, and the ruins of a Danish sugar plantation at the trailhead. The campground behind the beach reopened after Hurricane Irma restoration. More room to spread out than Trunk Bay. The plantation ruins are a five-minute walk and worth the detour before you hit the sand.

3. Honeymoon Beach

Caneel BayFreeWalk-in or water taxi

A 10-minute flat trail from the Caneel Bay parking area, or a $12 round-trip water taxi from Cruz Bay. The barrier keeps it uncrowded. White sand, calm water, a beach bar with food and drinks, and good snorkeling on the south side. One of the few beaches on St. John with a full bar right on the sand. The old Caneel Bay resort behind it is still closed — eerie and interesting to glimpse from the trail.

4. Maho Bay

North ShoreFreeSea turtles

The best place in the USVI to swim with sea turtles. Green turtles feed on the sea grass in the shallows — wade in knee-deep and wait. Calm, shallow water makes it ideal for kids. No facilities except roadside parking. The beach is narrow but the turtle experience makes it unforgettable. Best turtle sightings in the morning when the water is still. Bring your own snorkel gear.

Hiking

5. Reef Bay Trail

South Shore2.2 miles one-wayModerate

The signature hike on St. John. A downhill trail through tropical forest past sugar mill ruins and Taino petroglyphs carved into rock pools, ending at a beach. The National Park Service runs a guided hike with a boat ride back to Cruz Bay ($40, book at the visitor center — sells out fast). Going on your own means hiking back uphill, which is tough in the heat. Start early either way. The petroglyphs alone are worth the trip — pre-Columbian carvings in natural stone pools filled with rainwater.

6. Ram Head Trail

South Shore1.8 miles round tripModerate

Starts at Salt Pond Bay beach and climbs to a dramatic cliff with 360-degree views of the Caribbean, St. Croix in the distance, and the British Virgin Islands. Exposed and hot — bring plenty of water and go early or late. The trail ends at a rocky point that feels like the edge of the world. One of the best sunset spots in the USVI if you time it right. Combine it with a swim at Salt Pond before or after.

7. Lind Point Trail

Cruz Bay1.1 miles one-wayEasy

Starts behind the National Park visitor center in Cruz Bay and leads to Honeymoon Beach or an overlook above Caneel Bay. Easy, shaded, and doable in sandals. A good option when you're killing time before a ferry or want a short walk with a view. The overlook spur takes 15 minutes and gives you a postcard view of Caneel Bay and St. Thomas across the water.

Hiking tip

Start before 9 AM — shade disappears fast and the heat is serious by midday. Bring at least a liter of water per person. Trail maps are available at the National Park visitor center in Cruz Bay (open 8 AM – 4:30 PM). Wear real shoes for Reef Bay and Ram Head; the rest are fine in sport sandals.

Water Activities

8. Snorkeling at Waterlemon Cay

Leinster BayFreeBest reef

The best snorkeling on St. John — possibly the best in the USVI. A 15-minute shore walk from the Annaberg parking area, then a short swim to a small offshore island with a reef teeming with fish, turtles, rays, and occasional nurse sharks. Current can be tricky — swim around the cay counterclockwise (with the current). Almost no crowd because the walk filters out casual visitors. Bring your own gear; nothing available for rent. Check our USVI snorkeling guide for more spots.

9. Kayak and Snorkel Tours

Various$70-120Guided

Guided kayak-and-snorkel combos paddle through mangroves and along coastline before snorkeling a reef. Most launch from Cruz Bay or Caneel Bay. Half-day tours run 3-4 hours and include gear. A good way to see parts of the coast you can't reach by land. Our kayak and snorkel guide covers the best operators and routes.

10. Full-Day Boat Charter

Cruz Bay$600-1,500Private

Private or small-group boats take you to beaches and snorkel spots unreachable by road — the BVI, remote south shore coves, or a loop around the island. Most include lunch, drinks, and snorkel gear. Split among a group of 4-6, the per-person cost is comparable to a resort excursion with a fraction of the crowd. Book through Cruz Bay watersports operators or your villa rental company.

Culture and Food

11. Annaberg Sugar Plantation Ruins

North ShoreFree w/ park pass30 min

The best-preserved sugar plantation ruins in the USVI. Windmill tower, slave quarters, factory buildings, and interpretive signs explaining the brutal history of sugar production in the Danish West Indies. A self-guided loop trail takes 20-30 minutes. The ruins sit on a hill with views of Leinster Bay and the British Virgin Islands. Combine with a snorkel at Waterlemon Cay, which starts from the same parking area.

12. Cruz Bay Food Scene

Cruz Bay$$-$$$Walkable

Cruz Bay packs a surprising amount of good food into a few blocks. The Longboard for seafood tacos and frozen drinks, Extra Virgin Bistro for upscale Caribbean-Italian, Lime Inn for fresh catch, and Our Market Smoothies for acai bowls and juices. For local food on a budget, hit the food trucks near the ferry dock — roti, pates, and johnnycakes. Most restaurants don't take reservations; arrive before 6:30 PM in high season or expect a wait.

13. Mongoose Junction

Cruz BayShoppingLocal art

A stone courtyard shopping complex with galleries, jewelry stores, and boutiques — all locally owned, no chains. Best for Caribbean art, handmade jewelry, and gifts that aren't mass-produced tourist junk. Donald Schnell Studio has blown glass, Bajo el Sol gallery features Caribbean painters, and Bougainvillea Boutique carries resort wear. Five minutes on foot from the ferry dock.

14. Stargazing

South ShoreFreeAfter dark

Minimal light pollution makes St. John one of the best stargazing spots in the eastern US. Salt Pond Bay parking area and the Ram Head trailhead are dark enough to see the Milky Way clearly. No equipment needed — just lie on the hood of your car and look up. Best viewing from December through April when humidity is lowest. New moon nights are dramatically better than full moon.

15. Sunset at the Battery

Cruz BayFreeDaily ritual

The old Danish battery overlooking Cruz Bay harbor is where locals and visitors gather to watch the sunset. Grab a drink from a nearby bar, find a spot on the wall, and watch the sky turn orange over St. Thomas. It's a nightly ritual — informal, free, and the best way to end a day on St. John. The National Park visitor center is right next to it if you want to combine the visit.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is St. John worth visiting as a day trip?

Yes — a day trip from St. Thomas gives you enough time for two beaches and lunch in Cruz Bay. Take the first ferry from Red Hook (7:15 AM), hit Trunk Bay and Cinnamon Bay, eat in Cruz Bay, and catch a late afternoon ferry back. For hiking or exploring beyond North Shore beaches, an overnight stay is better.

How do you get to St. John USVI?

Passenger ferry from Red Hook, St. Thomas ($7.50 each way, every hour from 6 AM to midnight). The ride takes 20 minutes. A car ferry also runs from Red Hook if you want to bring a vehicle ($50 round trip plus the passenger fare). There is no airport on St. John.

Do you need a car on St. John?

Not for a day trip — open-air taxis run from Cruz Bay to all North Shore beaches ($7-14 per person). For multi-day stays, a rental car or Jeep gives you freedom to explore south shore trails and remote beaches. Book early in peak season — the island has limited rental inventory. Drive on the left.

What is St. John known for?

Two-thirds of St. John is protected as Virgin Islands National Park, making it one of the most pristine islands in the Caribbean. It is known for world-class beaches (especially Trunk Bay), excellent hiking trails through tropical forest, and a quieter, more natural atmosphere compared to St. Thomas.

Is St. John expensive?

St. John is the most expensive USVI island. Restaurant meals run $20-50, vacation rentals start around $200/night, and groceries cost 30-50% more than the mainland since everything is shipped in. Budget tips: pack lunch for beach days, buy provisions at Starfish Market, and visit on a day trip from cheaper St. Thomas.
