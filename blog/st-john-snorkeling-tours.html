St. John Snorkeling Tours & Excursions: Best Spots & Boats

Ready to book? Browse all USVI snorkeling tours with live prices and open dates.

WanderUSVI editorial · Published 2026-07-14

If you've never snorkeled before — or you've only tried it once or twice at a crowded pool — the idea of swimming in open water can feel intimidating. Here's the good news: St. John, USVI, is one of the most forgiving places in the Caribbean for beginners. The bays are shallow, the water is typically calm, and the marine life swims right up to you. You don't need certification, you don't need expensive gear, and you don't need prior experience.

This guide covers the best snorkeling tours in St. John specifically suited for first-timers — the calmest bays, what to expect, and how to pick an operator who knows how to make newcomers feel comfortable.

Why St. John has the best snorkeling in the USVI

Two-thirds of St. John is Virgin Islands National Park, so the north-shore bays — Trunk, Cinnamon, Maho, Leinster — have had protected reef and seagrass for 60 years. That means turtles over the grass at Maho, intact hard coral at Waterlemon Cay, and a marked underwater trail at Trunk Bay with signs naming what you're looking at. Everything is within 20 minutes of Cruz Bay by boat.

The best St. John snorkeling spots

Trunk Bay — the famous one: a 225-yard underwater trail, lifeguards, gear rental, $5 park fee. Go before 10am on cruise days. Waterlemon Cay — a small cay off Leinster Bay; reach it by a 0.8-mile flat trail from the Annaberg parking lot, then a 150-yard swim. Best coral on the island, some current on the outside. Maho Bay — calm and shallow, the most reliable place to float over turtles. Honeymoon Beach — a boat-tour staple on St. John's west side with easy entry. Cinnamon Bay — a long beach with a small cay to swim around. Shore access works for all of these; boats add the offshore cays and two stops in one trip.

Boat tour vs shore snorkeling

Shore snorkeling is free beyond the park fee and gear ($12–15/day in Cruz Bay). Boat excursions (about $75–$130 per person for a half day) add sites you can't walk to — Lovango Cay, Carvel Rock, the back side of Waterlemon — plus a guide in the water, which is what makes the difference for first-timers. Kayak-and-snorkel combos run 3 hours and pair a mangrove paddle with a turtle snorkel.

When to go

Water is 79–84°F year-round. December–April has the calmest mornings and the biggest crowds; May–June and November are the quiet sweet spot. Afternoon trade winds chop up the north shore, so morning tours see more.

St. John snorkeling questions

What are the best snorkeling tours in St. John?

Half-day boat excursions from Cruz Bay that hit two north-shore sites — National Park snorkel trips, turtle snorkels at Maho, and kayak-and-snorkel combos through the mangroves. The tours on this page are the top-rated bookable options.

Where is the best snorkeling in St. John?

Waterlemon Cay for coral and starfish, Maho Bay for turtles, Trunk Bay for the marked underwater trail and easy facilities. All three are inside Virgin Islands National Park.

Can you snorkel St. John from St. Thomas?

Yes. Take the Red Hook ferry (20 minutes) to Cruz Bay and join a tour there, or book a St. Thomas boat that snorkels St. John's north shore — most do.

Do St. John snorkeling excursions include gear?

Yes — mask, snorkel, fins and a flotation vest are standard on boat trips, and guides give a briefing before the first stop. Rent gear in Cruz Bay only if you're snorkeling from shore.
