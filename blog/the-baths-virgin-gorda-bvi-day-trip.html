The Baths, Virgin Gorda & BVI Day Trips from USVI

Updated October 2026 · WanderUSVI

The British Virgin Islands sit within sight of the US Virgin Islands — on a clear day you can see Tortola from the hills above Charlotte Amalie. But they are a different country, with their own customs, their own pace, and their own landmark: The Baths at Virgin Gorda, a geological spectacle unlike anything else in the Caribbean. For USVI visitors with an extra day, crossing over to the BVI is one of the best things you can do.

The crossing requires a passport and a customs stop, but day trip boats from St. Thomas and St. John handle the paperwork as part of the itinerary. The BVI's most famous single site — The Baths — is a 60- to 90-minute boat ride from the USVI, and the experience justifies every minute of the trip.

What Are The Baths?

The Baths are at the southern tip of Virgin Gorda, the third-largest island in the BVI. The geological story starts roughly 70 million years ago, when granite plutons intruded into the seafloor. Over time, erosion exposed the granite and broke it into enormous rounded boulders — some as large as two-story houses — that now sit stacked and tumbled along the shoreline. Seawater fills the passages between them, creating a network of pools, tunnels, and shaded grottos that you wade and scramble through.

The main trail enters The Baths from the north beach, threads through the boulder labyrinth, and emerges at Devil's Bay — a calm, sheltered white sand beach on the south side of the formation, accessible only through The Baths or by boat. The walking distance is short, perhaps a quarter mile, but the route involves climbing, ducking, and wading in shallow pools, making it genuinely fun rather than just scenic.

Beyond The Baths

Most day trips from the USVI include more than just The Baths. Common stops include The Caves at Norman Island — a cluster of sea caves on the west side of Norman Island where pirate treasure legends persist and snorkeling inside the cave mouths is excellent — and The Indians, a cluster of rock pinnacles off Norman Island that host spectacular reef on all sides. Some trips include Jost Van Dyke, a small island with a handful of legendary beach bars along Great Harbour.

The BVI sailing scene is one of the most famous in the world, and day trips by catamaran or powerboat give a real sense of why: the passage between the islands is deep blue, the anchorages are calm, and the density of good snorkeling and beaches per mile of coastline is extraordinary.

Practical Notes

A valid US passport or passport card is required. All passengers clear BVI customs on arrival, typically at Virgin Gorda Yacht Harbour or Soper's Hole on Tortola. Day trip operators based in St. Thomas or St. John handle the customs process as part of the booking. BVI charges a departure tax per person; most day trips include this in the ticket price, but confirm when booking.

Local tip: The Baths are most crowded mid-morning when multiple day trip boats arrive at the same time. Trips that depart early from the USVI and arrive first have noticeably better conditions — fewer people in the grottos, better light for photos, calmer water in the pools.
